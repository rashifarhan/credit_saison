Zoho's pitch is simple: one ecosystem, every business app you'll ever need. The reality, once you're actually inside it, is 50-plus separate apps, each with its own pricing page, and a bill that creeps up every time you turn one more app on. CRM is one price. Add People for HR, that's another subscription. Add Books for accounting, a third.

None of that makes Zoho bad. It makes Zoho a platform built for a company that's ready to manage a platform. A lot of small businesses aren't — they just want sales, HR, and invoicing to work, today, without someone's full-time job being "Zoho administrator."

If that's you, here are four real alternatives, compared honestly, not just a list of logos. (Looking at free options specifically? See free CRM software in India and our best free CRM software in India roundup.)

Why People Actually Go Looking for a Zoho Alternative

Three reasons come up constantly, in roughly this order.

The per-app pricing adds up faster than expected. Zoho CRM Standard runs about $14/user/month. Add Zoho People for HR and Zoho Books for accounting, and a 10-person team can land at $400–600/month before anyone's used a single "advanced" feature.

The free tier is narrower than it looks. Zoho CRM's free plan caps out at 3 users and strips out email integration and forecasting — exactly the features a growing sales team notices missing first.

Too many separate logins for too few people. A 6-person company doesn't need CRM, HR, and accounting to live in three different apps with three different permission systems. It needs one place where a closed deal becomes an invoice without anyone re-typing a customer's name.

How the Four Actually Differ

Odoo is Zoho's closest cousin in spirit — a modular suite where you pick apps and pay per app, per user. If what frustrates you about Zoho is the interface rather than the per-app pricing model, Odoo won't fix that; it's the same model with a different UI.

HubSpot earns its reputation on the marketing side. Its CRM is genuinely well built. But HR and accounting live outside HubSpot entirely, so switching from Zoho to HubSpot swaps one kind of sprawl for another — you'd still need separate tools for the rest of the business. When Zoho Is Still the Right Call

Don't switch off Zoho if you've already got 3+ Zoho apps wired together with Zoho Flow automations, or if your business genuinely needs one of its deeper, more specialized apps — Zoho Inventory's multi-warehouse tracking or Zoho Books' advanced multi-currency handling, for example. Ripping out an integrated Zoho setup to save on per-app cost usually isn't worth the migration pain if it's already working.

Is there a truly free Zoho alternative for small business?
EzNxT is free for up to 10 users across CRM, HR, and accounting together. HubSpot's CRM is free but caps at 2 users and doesn't include HR or accounting. Freshworks' free tier is capped at 3 agents on one product only.

What's the cheapest way to replace Zoho One for a 10-person team?
Zoho One itself (the bundled suite) runs roughly $37/user/month if you're on it. For a 10-person team, that's about $4,440/year. EzNxT covers the equivalent CRM+HR+accounting ground free for the first 10 users.

Does switching from Zoho mean losing my data?
No, but plan the move rather than doing it overnight. Export your contacts, deals, and invoice history as CSV from each Zoho app, pick a cut-off date, and bring that data into the new system from there. Keep Zoho data accessible for reference during the transition.
